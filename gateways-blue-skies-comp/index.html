Competition 2022

NASA Gateways to Blue Skies

Abstract

The NASA Gateways to Blue Skies competition challenged student teams to envision the future of aviation. In response to the increasing threat of climate change, NASA has declared that it aims to cut carbon emissions by half by 2050 compared to 2005, and achieve net zero emissions in 2060. To meet this goal, airports, airlines, and other organizations in the aviation industry will have to reduce emissions in a major way. Our paper focuses on how airports can be designed to support future changes in the aviation industry with a specific emphasis on two themes: airport sustainability and autonomy. Each theme is tied to our airport innovations and guided our final design of the airport.

To start, we modeled our initial concepts on the Denver International Airport. The new technologies we describe center around changes in aircraft formand fuel type, gate design, urban airmobility (UAM), storing multiple fuel types, and autonomous systems. Even though our concepts are applied to the Denver International Airport, we believe the designs can be generalized to other airports as well.

In this effort, our team won first place and was invited to present our proposal at the Airports Council International (ACI) - North America conference in Minneapolis in 2022.
