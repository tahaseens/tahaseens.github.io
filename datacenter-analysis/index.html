Data Center Energy Dashboard

A data center energy accountability platform for Loudoun County, Virginia

Overview

Loudoun County has one of the largest concentrations of data centers in the world but there is no publically available tool that enables residents to review data center growth, energy demand, and community impact in one place. As AI expands into the daily life of millions around the world, it is only expected that regions like Loudoun County will be the first affected by the massive expansion and absorb the costs (grid strain, water use, noise, land, etc).

This independent project is designed to build both a working technical tool and a policy analysis paper to close that gap. The pipeline will pull from public permit records, Dominion Energy IRP filings, EPA eGrid data, and FOIA-obtained water usage figures, etc in order to display relevant data. The dashboard models projected energy demand, carbon intensity, and the downstream effects of new hyperscale facilities through 2030. A physical sensor component may be added to further support the dashboard with real time data.

The policy paper will evaluate the externalities given the data outcomes from the information collection and physical sensor output. Furthermore, the paper will outline concrete recommendations on mandatory disclosure, community benefit agreements, and grid impact fees. Targeting Energy Research & Social Science Journal or the Bipartisan Policy Center for submission.

Timeline
Phase 1
Data Pipeline
Permit records, Dominion Energy IRP filings, EPA eGrid emissions, and water consumption via FOIA aggregated into a reproducible open-source Python pipeline.
Phase 2
Public Dashboard
Interactive scenario modeling for energy demand, carbon intensity, hyperscale growth projections through 2030. Built for county planners, journalists, and advocacy groups.
Phase 3
Physical AQI Sensor
ROS2 + Raspberry Pi deployed near the data center corridor, feeding real-time environmental (likely AQI) data directly into the dashboard.
Phase 4
Policy Paper
Paper output evaluating externalities of the empirical findings. Concrete recommendations on disclosure, community benefit agreements, and grid impact fees.
